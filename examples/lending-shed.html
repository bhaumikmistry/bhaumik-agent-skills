12Minutes of use, across the whole life of the average domestic drill.

There is roughly one power drill per household in Britain, and over its entire working life the average one runs for about twelve minutes. The drill was never the point. The hole was.

Marston has around 9,000 households. That is a great many drills sitting in cupboards doing nothing, while somebody two streets away buys another one for a job that takes an afternoon. The Lending Shed is 640 tools in one unit on Wharf Road, shared by 1,180 members who pay a subscription and sign for what they take.

What this is, and what it is not

Two assumptions get made about us within about four seconds of someone hearing the words “tool library”. Both are wrong, and both are worth clearing up before you read a price.

Not a charity for people who cannot afford tools

Our members include two architects, a retired GP, a good number of landlords and at least one man with a fully kitted garage who joined purely for the floor sander. Nobody is means-tested. There is a lower rate, it is self-declared, and we do not ask for proof.

A subscription library that happens to lend tools

You pay an annual membership. That gives you borrowing rights over a catalogue of 640 tools. Loans themselves are free within your allowance; a refundable deposit is held per item and released when the item comes back. It works like a library because it is one.

Not a scam, and not a hire shop in disguise

We are a Community Benefit Society, registration 8241, owned by our members. Accounts are published every March and pinned on the noticeboard by the door. Nobody takes a dividend. Six part-time staff, forty-odd volunteers, no shareholders.

Cheaper than hire, and slower than buying

A weekend hire on an SDS hammer runs to about £45 in Marston. Here, £45 is the whole year. In exchange we are open four days a week, the popular kit gets booked out, and you will sometimes wait until Thursday. That trade is the actual deal.

Deposits, late fees and breakage: the part most places bury

A tool library only works if things come back, in one piece, roughly on time. Ours mostly do. The rules below exist for the small number of times they do not, and every one of them is capped. There is no clause on this page that can cost you more than the deposit you put down.

Deposits are held, not charged. It is a card authorisation, released the day the item comes back. Nothing is taken up front.

If it is late

Two days’ grace on Bands A and B, then the daily fee starts, and it stops the moment it reaches the deposit. Ring us before the due date and we will move it: a renewal costs nothing at all if nobody is booked in after you.

Why this existsThe floor sander is booked eleven weekends deep by March. A tool kept an extra week is not an abstraction, it is somebody else’s half-stripped living room.

If it breaks

Tell us when you bring it back and the repair is capped at the deposit, whatever the tool originally cost us. Say nothing, and let the next borrower discover the fault, and you cover the full repair and we have a conversation about it. In practice, almost everybody tells us.

Why this existsA hidden fault gets found by a stranger, mid-job, occasionally dangerously. We would far rather absorb the cost than have that happen.

If it is simply worn

Blunt blades, stretched belts, scuffed cases, worn carbon brushes, a chewed extension lead: never charged for, ever. That is what use looks like, and it is priced into the membership.

Why this existsIf people are frightened of ordinary wear they use the tool timidly, or they go and buy their own, and then we have not solved anything.

Consumables

Things designed to be used up are charged at what we pay for them: sanding belts £3.20, a 60-tooth TCT blade £11, mixer fuel at the pump price. The full list is on the counter and on the booking page. Bring your own instead if you would rather.

Why this existsA floor sander gets through £25 of abrasive in one hallway. Spreading that across everyone would mean members who never sand a floor paying for the ones who do.

If it never comes back

After 30 days and three attempts to reach you, the item is written off, the deposit is kept and your membership is suspended until you come and talk to us. It is not sent to a debt agency and it does not touch your credit file.

Why this existsWe lost nine items this way last year out of 2,140 loans. Suspension is the entire enforcement mechanism, and it turns out to be enough.

Your own safety

You are borrowing a machine, and using it properly is on you. Band C carries an induction for exactly that reason. Everything leaves the counter PAT-tested with its guards fitted, and if a guard is missing, refuse the tool and tell us so we can take it off the shelf.

Why this existsIt is the same reason we will not hand you a chainsaw on a Saturday morning because you fancy having a go at the sycamore.

Things we will never do

  • Charge you more than the deposit for damage. No exceptions, and no small print underneath this sentence.
  • Take the deposit up front. It is a card hold, released on return.
  • Auto-renew your membership without emailing you a fortnight beforehand, with a one-click way to stop it.
  • Pass a debt to a collection agency, or record anything against your credit file.
  • Ask you to evidence your income for the concessionary rate.

The repair bench, Tuesdays from six

Every tool that comes back damaged goes onto a bench at the back of the unit, and on Tuesday evenings eleven volunteers work through the queue. Ade spent thirty years on small-engine service. Priya does the electrical testing. The rest is whoever turns up.

This is why breakage does not sink us. A pressure washer with a split pump housing is a £220 replacement at retail and a £31 part on the bench. A member who breaks something is, from where we sit, mostly an evening’s work rather than a loss.

Bring your own broken things along too. If it is small, electrical or petrol, somebody will look at it, free, while you wait. We fix roughly two in three.

How a loan actually goes

  1. Join at the counter

    Ten minutes. Name, address, and a card for deposit holds. No credit check and no references.

  2. Book it, or chance it

    Reserve online up to three weeks ahead, or walk in. Saturdays are busy. Thursday mornings are empty.

  3. Sign it out

    We test it works in front of you, hand over the case and consumables, and place the deposit hold.

  4. Bring it back

    A quick condition check on the counter, hold released the same day. Tell us anything that went wrong.

Fair questions, asked at the counter most weeks

So what is the catch?

Availability and opening hours. If you need a tile cutter at eight on a Sunday evening, we are shut and a hire shop is not. We open Tuesday evening, Thursday, Friday and Saturday. Popular Band C items book out weeks ahead in spring. That is the honest cost of paying £45 a year instead of £45 a weekend.

I am not confident with tools. Is this for me?

It is the main reason people join. Staff will show you how a thing works before it leaves the counter, and nobody has ever been judged here for asking which way round a jigsaw blade goes. The Tuesday bench is also where people come to be shown things properly.

What if I break something expensive?

You pay the repair up to the value of the deposit and not a penny beyond it, even if the tool is beyond saving. Telling us on return is what keeps that cap in place. Sixty-one items came back damaged last year, and the total charged to members across all of them was £1,410.

Can I use the tools for paid work?

On Workshop membership, yes, within reason, and on the understanding that our insurance covers the tool rather than your job. We ask trade members not to monopolise the six items that are permanently in demand. Two people have been asked to ease off; both took it well.

Do you take donated tools?

Gladly, and about a third of the catalogue arrived this way. Anything electrical is PAT-tested before it reaches a shelf. If we already hold six of a thing we will say so and point you at the Marston Repair Café rather than quietly putting it in a skip.

Who actually owns all this?

The members do. We are a Community Benefit Society, registration 8241, one member one vote, with a board elected at the AGM each March. Nobody can sell it or take a profit out of it. The annual accounts go on the noticeboard and to anyone who emails and asks for them.
