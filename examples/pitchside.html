The decision was right and it was made early. It just never became something anyone could see. That last line is the whole reason Pitchside exists.

How it works

Three taps, standing on the grass, with cold hands.

Step one

Open the pitch you are standing on

The link in your Saturday morning text opens straight to today's fixtures. No password at 7am, no app store, no update waiting to install.

Step two

Mark it, and say why in five words

Three buttons, each the size of a thumb. A reason is optional but it is the thing that stops the phone ringing all morning. Add a photo of the goalmouth if it will save an argument.

Step three

Everyone on that fixture is told

Text and email, inside a minute: the home squad, the away club's secretary and squad, the referee, and the league. Away teams pay nothing and install nothing to be on the list.

The record

Who decided what, and when. Written down before anyone asks.

Every mark carries a name, a time and a pitch. When the league queries a late call-off in March, the answer is a page you can send them, not four people trying to remember February.

Built for the person walking the pitch

Not for a phone in an office. For a phone in a wet coat pocket.

  • Buttons you can hit in gloves.Nothing on the marking screen is smaller than a thumb, and there is no confirm dialogue to fumble.
  • Works on one bar of signal.If the mark cannot send, it queues and goes when the signal returns. The time on the record is the moment you tapped, not the moment it sent.
  • Nothing to install, for anybody.Groundskeepers open a link. Players and referees receive a text. That is the entire technical requirement.
  • Ten minutes to change your mind.A mark can be pulled back for ten minutes without a fuss. After that a correction is posted, and both entries stay on the record.
  • Readable in bad light.Heavy type, strong contrast, no pale grey small print, and it turns dark automatically when your phone does.
  • Only named people can mark.The club secretary decides who holds that authority for each pitch, and can hand it over on a Friday night when someone is away.

Questions we get asked

Straight answers.

Does it decide for us, using weather data?

No, and it never will. A forecast does not know that your north goalmouth holds water for two days after rain. The person standing on it does. Pitchside carries a human decision — it does not make one.

Our groundskeeper does not really do apps.

There is no app. He gets a text on Saturday morning with a link; the link opens three buttons. If he would still rather ring someone, he rings the secretary and she marks it — the record then shows that she marked it on his call, which is more than anyone can prove today.

What do players have to do?

Nothing. They receive a text. There is no account to make, no password to forget, and no group chat to be added to.

What if we mark it off and then it dries out?

You have ten minutes to pull a mark back before anything is sent as final. After that you post a new mark, everyone is told again, and both entries stay in the log with their times. Changing your mind is normal; hiding that you changed it is what causes rows.

Who is allowed to call a pitch off?

Only the people the club secretary names, pitch by pitch. Authority can be handed to someone else in a couple of taps when the usual person is away, and that handover is itself recorded.

We are a five-team club with three pitches. Which one do we need?

The Club tier at £45 for the season. If you are unsure, start on the free tier with your first team this Saturday and move up when it has proved itself — nothing is lost in the move.

What happens to our data at the end of the season?

It stays yours. The decision log is exportable at any time, phone numbers are used only to send match notices, and nothing is sold to anyone. If you leave, we delete the numbers and you keep the export.
