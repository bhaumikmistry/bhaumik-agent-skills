# Question Craft

How to write questions that get real answers, not LinkedIn bios.

## Philosophy

A great question does three things:
1. **References something specific** the person actually did
2. **Creates a tension or contrast** they have to resolve
3. **Invites a story**, not a lecture

The goal: make them say something they haven't said before.

## Question anatomy

```
[Specific reference to their work] + [tension/contrast] + [open-ended invitation]
```

**Bad:** "What's your creative process?"
**Good:** "That toxic songs post starts with 'kal drive kar raha tha.' We've ALL had that moment. But you wrote it down. What happens in between?"

**Bad:** "How do you balance everything?"
**Good:** "You're 22, in banking hours, running a brand, angel investing, writing books. What have you already learned about what's sustainable vs. what just looks productive?"

## The arc (6-8 questions as a narrative)

| # | Purpose | Pattern |
|---|---------|---------|
| 1 | **Origin/Spark** | How did they get here? What starts them? (not "tell me about yourself") |
| 2 | **The Craft** | How do they actually do the thing? Process, day-to-day, unglamorous reality |
| 3 | **Philosophy/Contrarian** | What do they believe that most people in their world disagree with? |
| 4 | **Tension/Contradiction** | The thing that seems at odds in their life or work |
| 5 | **Identity/Human** | Culture, burnout, who they build for, what drives them |
| 6 | **Sustainability** | How they hold it all. What breaks. What they say no to. |
| 7 | **Growth/Mile markers** | How do they know they're getting better? (no titles/metrics) |
| 8 | **Closing/Forward** | Advice to past self, or what they're betting on next |

Pick 5-7 that fit. You don't need all 8.

## Techniques

### Build on the answer
Pick up their EXACT words and go deeper:
- They say "gut feel" → ask what that gut feel actually feels like
- They say "before we called em agents" → ask what they were building back then
- They give a short answer → use humor to push for more ("you gave me the plain white t-shirt answer, I know there's an Indiya NYC piece in there")

### Create productive discomfort
Ask about the contradiction:
- "You evaluate businesses at BofA all day, then go home and build taste-first things that won't scale. Does the IB lens help or hurt?"
- "You were at Amazon (trains on everything) then left to build consent-first AI. Was that a reaction?"

### One question, not three
If you have two angles, pick the sharper one. Never stack.

**Bad:** "How did you start, and what was hard, and what kept you going?"
**Good:** "What made The Gen-Z Book cross from concept to 'I'm finishing this no matter what'?"

### Use their work as the hook
Don't ask abstract questions. Anchor in something specific they made:
- A blog post they wrote
- A product they shipped
- A specific career transition
- An exact quote from their site

### When they give short answers
Don't just re-ask. Reframe with humor and specificity:
- "Would love to hear more — can you think of a specific moment where that happened?"
- "That's the white t-shirt answer, give me the handblock-printed version 😄"

## For the full question list format

When someone asks for all questions at once (Rahul, Shruti style):
- Number them Q1-Q8
- Each self-contained but building on a narrative
- Add: "Answer whenever, however much. Skip any that don't resonate."
- Add: "But selfishly, I hope you answer them all 😄"

## Anti-patterns

- 🚫 Generic questions ("What advice would you give?")
- 🚫 Flattery disguised as questions ("You're so amazing at X, how?")
- 🚫 Questions they've answered publicly — read their posts first
- 🚫 Too many sub-questions in one
- 🚫 Leading questions ("Don't you think that...")
- 🚫 Starting at their current role — go to the origin
