# Outreach

How to draft cold outreach that gets a reply.

## The formula that works

```
[Catchy opening — the discovery story] →
[Who you are — one line] →
[Why them — specific, not flattery] →
[The ask — clear, easy, low pressure] →
[Optional: first question included]
```

## Step 1: Research first

Before writing anything:
- [ ] Read their full LinkedIn (transitions, not just current role)
- [ ] Check their blog/website/Twitter for real writing
- [ ] Find 2-3 specific things to reference (not generic)
- [ ] Identify the tension or contradiction in their path
- [ ] Check if they've answered similar questions publicly (don't waste their time)

## Step 2: Find your hook

The opening line determines if they read the rest. Best hooks:

**The rabbit hole story:**
> "So here's what happened. I found your website, left a comment, realized it wasn't saving properly, opened a PR on your repo, and now here I am emailing you because apparently I have no chill."

**The scroll-down discovery:**
> "I'm scrolling your LinkedIn and I see [current thing]. Cool. Then I scroll to [year] and hit [completely different career]. Wait what?"

**The emotional hit:**
> "So here's what happened. I was scrolling, half paying attention, and then [specific piece] came up. Full stop. Phone down. Teary eyes. And now I'm here writing you this email at work."

**The shared obsession:**
> "That [specific object/thing] on your site? I can't stop thinking about it. I'm a [same-interest] nerd and that one hit different."

**The contradiction/energy:**
> "There are thousands of [their role]. Most of them [do the normal thing]. You're out here [doing the exceptional version]. That's not normal energy."

## Step 3: Introduce yourself (one line max)

> "I'm Bhaumik — engineer at Amazon, and on the side I talk to people about how they do what they do for a series called Zero Token, All Human."

That's it. Don't over-explain. Link to the site. Link to one published interview as an example of what it looks like.

## Step 4: The ask

Keep it:
- Clear: "Would love to ask you a few questions"
- Easy: "Fully async, over email, reply whenever"
- Low pressure: "No pressure. Even two lines is cool."
- Optional out: "If not, no worries / zero hard feelings"

## Step 5: Include Q1 or don't

**Include Q1 when:**
- You've already done something to earn their attention (PR, comment, prior interaction)
- The person seems busy and you want to lower the reply barrier

**Don't include Q1 when:**
- It's a stranger on DMs (keep it short, ask for email)
- They need to opt in first

## Platform-specific adjustments

| Platform | Approach |
|----------|----------|
| Email (cold) | Full outreach + Q1. ~150-200 words max. |
| Email (via intro) | Thank the connector, include forwardable block, Q1 optional |
| Twitter/X DM | Short. Hook + one line about series + ask for email. |
| Instagram DM | Even shorter. Personal connection + ask for email. |
| LinkedIn DM | Medium length. Professional-adjacent but still friendly. |
| Text (you have their number) | Super casual. "hey! sending you an email — would love to chat about X" |

## Via-intro format

When someone agrees to introduce you:

```
[Short thank-you to the connector]
---
[Forwardable block addressed to the target]
[Self-contained — reads perfectly when forwarded]
[No inside references the connector would need to explain]
[Include who you are, why them, the ask]
```

## The "already knows you" format

When you've met them IRL (conference, event, cricket game):
- Skip the long intro — they know you're real
- Reference the shared context immediately
- Go straight to why you're reaching out now
- Can be more direct with the ask

## What kills outreach

- Being too long (over 200 words)
- Sounding like a template
- Listing their resume back at them
- Using "I'd love to pick your brain"
- Not referencing anything specific about them
- Asking without giving (engage with their work first when possible)
- Starting with "Hey, I'm [name]. I'm a..." — tell a story first
- Using dashes and bold headers that make it look structured/AI-written
