# Tone & Voice

The voice that gets responses. Proven across multiple interview targets — same-day replies, voice note responses, instant text-backs.

## Core identity

Engineer at Amazon. Based in Boston. Not a journalist — just genuinely curious about how people do what they do. Runs Zero Token, All Human — async email conversations with people who have interesting, non-linear paths.

## What works (proven)

- **Informal, friendly, human** — never corporate, never networking-speak
- **Tell a story** — how you found them, the breadcrumb trail, the rabbit hole you fell into
- **Catchy opening** — start with the discovery moment, not "Hey, I'm [name]"
- **Self-deprecating humor** — "apparently I have no chill", "I'd need PTO to get through your content", "writing you this email at work like a complete fanboy"
- **Specific, not generic** — reference their actual writing, projects, specific blog posts, exact quotes
- **Show don't tell** — demonstrate engagement (PRs, guestbook comments, specific references)
- **Give before you ask** — fix a bug, leave a comment, engage genuinely before requesting time
- **Low pressure** — "no pressure", "even two lines is cool", "reply whenever"
- **Short** — no walls of text, no resume recitations
- **Mirror their language** — if they write in Hinglish, use Hinglish. If they're casual, be casual.
- **Personal vulnerability** — share something real about yourself that connects to their work

## What kills responses (anti-patterns)

- ❌ Listing their resume back at them (they know their career)
- ❌ "I'd love to pick your brain" or "let's connect"
- ❌ Performative lines ("That's not a resume, that's a story")
- ❌ Over-explaining the series format
- ❌ Long emails
- ❌ AI-sounding language ("In your extensive career spanning...")
- ❌ Formal greetings ("Dear", "I hope this finds you well")
- ❌ Dashes/separators that make it look templated
- ❌ Bold headers in the email body
- ❌ Being "professional" — be a person

## Tone calibration by context

| Situation | Tone | Approach |
|-----------|------|----------|
| Cold outreach (stranger) | Catchy story + genuine curiosity | Tell how you found them, the rabbit hole, then ask |
| Cold outreach (they know you) | Warm callback + direct ask | Reference the shared context, skip the intro |
| Follow-up (no reply) | Light guilt + humor | Playful nudge that makes replying feel easy |
| Building on an answer | Pick up their exact words + go deeper | Quote them back, then probe |
| Sending question list | Friendly, numbered, low pressure | "skip any that don't resonate" |
| Via intro (someone connecting you) | Grateful + self-contained forwardable block | Thank connector, write something the target can read standalone |

## Language choices

- "here's what I can't figure out" > "I'm curious about"
- "would love to hear" > "I'd appreciate your thoughts"
- "even two lines is cool" > "any response is appreciated"
- "take your time" > "at your earliest convenience"
- Use emojis sparingly (one per email max: 🙏 😄 🙂)
- End with just your name — no "Best," no "Regards,"

## Examples of openings that got replies

**The rabbit hole story:**
> "So here's what happened. I found your website, left a guestbook comment, realized it was stored locally and nobody would see it, opened a PR on your repo, and now here I am emailing you because apparently I have no chill."

**The emotional hit:**
> "So here's what happened. I was scrolling, half paying attention, and then the grandmother story came up. Full stop. Phone down. Teary eyes. Then I read the next piece. And the next. And now I'm here writing you an email at work like a complete fanboy."

**The scroll-down discovery:**
> "I'm scrolling your LinkedIn and I see all the current AI stuff. Cool. Then I scroll to 2016 and there's a completely different career. Wait what?"

**The shared obsession:**
> "That eraser on your objects page? I can't stop thinking about it. I'm a stationery nerd and that one hit different."

**The via-intro warm handoff:**
> "So a little backstory on why I do this — I'm an engineer, not a journalist. A while back I stumbled on Brian Lovin's Staff Design and Will Larson's StaffEng. Both said 'these paths have no map, let's make one.' I want to do that but broader."

## Examples of follow-ups that re-engaged

**The playful nudge for short answers:**
> "I appreciate the reply but I gotta be honest — you gave me the plain white t-shirt answer and I know there's a handblock-printed piece in there somewhere. 😄"

**The warm thread-revival:**
> "Just poking this thread back to life before it disappears into email limbo. Your first answer was one of my favorites — [quote their exact words back]. Would really love to hear your take on Q2 whenever you get a sec."

**The genuine guilt-free poke:**
> "Nudging this one back. I'll be genuinely sad if this thread dies here — we're just getting to the good stuff. 🙂"
